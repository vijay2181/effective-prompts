I want to learn **[CONCEPT]** from absolute zero.

Assume I know nothing about this topic. Explain it in **layman terms first**, as if you are teaching an intelligent beginner who has never encountered the concept before.

My goal is NOT just to get an answer. I want to:

1. Understand what it is.
2. Understand why it exists.
3. Understand what problem it solves.
4. Understand how it works internally.
5. Understand where and when it is used.
6. Understand how it connects to concepts I may already know.
7. Be able to explain it myself later without looking at notes.
8. Remember it for a very long time.

### Teaching rules

**Start from the absolute beginning.**
Do not assume prior knowledge. If another concept is required first, briefly teach that concept before continuing.

**Use a layered explanation:**

* Level 1: One-sentence explanation in very simple language.
* Level 2: Explain it using a real-world analogy.
* Level 3: Explain the actual technical concept in simple language.
* Level 4: Explain how it works step by step.
* Level 5: Show a realistic technical/example scenario.
* Level 6: Explain the important details and edge cases.
* Level 7: Explain what happens internally, if applicable.

For every important technical term:

* Give the term.
* Give its simple meaning.
* Explain why it matters.
* Give a concrete example.

### Build my mental model

Do not give me isolated facts.

Build a mental model where I can understand:
**What → Why → How → When → Where → What happens internally → What can go wrong → How to troubleshoot it.**

Explicitly point out:

* What I should remember.
* What I do NOT need to memorize.
* Common misconceptions.
* Similar concepts that are easy to confuse.
* The difference between related concepts.

### Make it practical

Use realistic examples rather than only definitions.

If the concept is technical:

* Show a simple example first.
* Then show a realistic production example.
* Explain every important part of the example.
* Show what happens step by step.
* Show common mistakes.
* Show how I would recognize and troubleshoot those mistakes.

If diagrams would help, create a simple ASCII diagram.

### Check my understanding

Do NOT simply finish with "Do you understand?"

Instead, teach one logical section at a time and periodically ask me short questions that test whether I actually understand it.

Prefer questions such as:

* "What do you think happens next?"
* "Why do you think this is needed?"
* "What would happen if we removed this?"
* "How is X different from Y?"
* "Explain this in your own words."

If my answer is wrong or incomplete:

1. Identify exactly where my mental model is wrong.
2. Explain the mistake.
3. Rebuild that part of the mental model.
4. Ask me another question to verify that I now understand it.

### Memory / long-term retention

At the end, create a **Memory Pack** containing:

1. **One-line definition**
2. **Core mental model**
3. **5–10 key points**
4. **Important relationships**
5. **Common confusions**
6. **Real-world example**
7. **"If you remember only 3 things..."**
8. **A tiny cheat sheet**
9. **5 recall questions**
10. **3 scenario-based questions**

Do not encourage me to memorize everything. Help me understand the underlying logic so that I can reconstruct the concept later.

### Active recall

After teaching, test me WITHOUT showing the answers.

Wait for my answers before revealing the correct answers.

Then evaluate my understanding and identify any weak areas.

### Spaced reinforcement

After I finish learning the concept, create a revision sequence for me:

* Review 1: shortly after learning
* Review 2: a few days later
* Review 3: about a week later
* Review 4: about a month later

For each review, give me only questions/prompts that force me to recall the concept rather than reread the explanation.

### Important

Do not overwhelm me by dumping everything at once.

Teach progressively.

If the topic is large, divide it into logical parts and tell me the roadmap first.

Use simple language, but do NOT oversimplify the actual technical meaning.

Whenever possible, connect new knowledge to previously explained concepts.

At the end of each major section, give me:
**"What you should now understand"**
and a very short recap.
